You are building a "Know Your Client" briefing for a bid team, from a tender's Strategy and
Context document. Your job is to surface what this document genuinely reveals about the buyer
(the client) — who they are, what matters to them, and anything the bid team should specifically
bear in mind before writing the response for THIS client, as opposed to a generic tender response.

Document text:
"""
{document_text}
"""

Task: decide whether this document genuinely discusses the client in a way that's useful for
briefing a bid team, then extract that content.

Rules:
- If it does, set client_info_found to true and provide:
  - client_summary: a short (2-4 sentence) synthesis of who this client is — organisation type,
    sector, structure, current situation, strategic priorities, whatever is genuinely discernible.
  - key_facts: a list of concrete, individually useful facts about the client (e.g. current
    suppliers or incumbents, budget context, organisational structure, key stakeholders, past
    issues or history with suppliers, regulatory or political context). Each item should be a
    short, standalone point.
  - considerations: a list of things the bid team should specifically keep in mind while writing
    this tender response for this client — sensitivities, stated preferences, tone or language
    expectations, known pain points to address, relationships to be mindful of, anything that
    would change how you'd pitch to this client versus a generic buyer.
- If the document does not genuinely discuss the client (for example, it is a scoring matrix, a
  bare procurement timetable, or a document that is entirely about the tender process with no real
  client detail), set client_info_found to false and leave client_summary, key_facts, and
  considerations empty. Do not invent or stretch client detail from unrelated content — reporting
  nothing found is always better than fabricating a client profile.
- Every point must be genuinely grounded in what the document actually says. Write key_facts and
  considerations in your own concise words — not verbatim copies — but never introduce a fact,
  name, or detail that isn't really supported by the source text.
